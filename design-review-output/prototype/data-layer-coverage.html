The three critical gaps
KBQs 4, 7, 8 — Positioning, Pricing, Access — and exactly what's missing.

These are the three KBQs scoring ≤ 2 of 5. They're the hardest to close because two of them (Pricing, Access) require licensed data — but they're also the KBQs that drive the largest commercial decisions in pharma. The exec who asks "how is competitor X reimbursed in our segment?" today gets nothing back from the platform.

KBQ 4 · Positioning · 1 / 5
What is the competitor saying about their drug?
Today the platform sees only the drug label text — the most heavily-regulated piece of messaging. It cannot see what the competitor is actually saying to physicians or patients.

The 28-source roadmap
Four phases. Sequenced by KBQ-impact and ingestion effort.

Phase 1 = free public sources we should already have (no licence cost). Phase 2 = the four paid sources whose licence cost is justified by the KBQ uplift. Phase 3 = international + private company depth. Phase 4 = the long tail of speciality + sentiment. Effort: S = 1-3 days, M = 1-2 weeks, L = 3+ weeks + ongoing licence.

The licensed-data conversation
Phase 2 · the four paid sources whose licence cost is justified.

Six paid sources in Phase 2; the four headline picks below turn KBQs 5, 7, 8 from "thin" to "comprehensive". Each is a six-figure annual commitment. The right move is to do the cost-benefit with the executive sponsor and pick the order based on the one TA where you most need full-stack CI.

IQVIA — sales by drug by month
L effort + ongoing
PAID · 6-figureLARGEKBQ 5 Sales
The canonical commercial input. TRx, NRx, share, by month, by geography. Closes 80% of KBQ 5 (Sales). Without it, "what share is the competitor gaining" is unanswerable. Most pharma companies already have it; integrating rather than re-implementing is the spec's stated approach.
MMIT — payer formularies + PA + step
L effort + ongoing
PAID · 6-figureLARGEKBQ 8 Access
The canonical access intelligence database. Closes 90% of KBQ 8 (Access) by itself. Combined with the free CMS Part D + Medicaid PDLs from Phase 1, this gives plan-level formulary granularity nationally.
AlphaSense — earnings transcripts + analyst
L effort + ongoing
PAID · 5-6 figureLARGEKBQ 5 Sales · KBQ 6 SWOT
Verbatim earnings call transcripts (Q&A) + sell-side analyst reports + sentiment. Closes the qualitative half of KBQ 5 that 8-K filings cannot. The Strategist agent's source-of-truth for "what is the CFO actually saying about Q3?".
RedBook (or FDB / Medi-Span) — WAC + AWP
L effort + ongoing
PAID · 5-6 figureLARGEKBQ 7 Pricing
Foundational US wholesale pricing. Closes the biggest KBQ 7 gap. Pairs with the free CMS pricing files from Phase 1 and HTA decisions from Phase 4 to give a full pricing picture.

Plus two smaller Phase 2 adds: Citeline (enriched trial + pipeline view, replaces some PubMed gap-filling, L effort) and Crunchbase / PitchBook (private-biotech financing for KBQ 2, M effort). Phase 3 adds 7 international sources (Health Canada, PMDA, TGA, NMPA, CDSCO, JapicCTI/CTRI/ChiCTR/ANZCTR, GLEIF). Phase 4 adds 7 speciality sources (ASCO/ESMO/ASH/AHA/ACC/AACR conference abstracts, Cochrane, EU EudraVigilance + WHO VigiBase, Sermo/Doximity, Drugs.com/AskAPatient/Reddit, iSpot.tv/Kantar, NICE/IQWIG/CADTH/SMC/ICER HTA). Full breakdown in the data-layer-audit.docx.

You have a strong evidence backbone for clinical and indication intelligence. To meet the Reimagined Spec's ambition of being a complete CI operating system, the next 28 connectors are not optional — they are the substrate that makes KBQs 4, 7, 8 answerable.
Three concrete next moves. Ship Phase 1 (the eight free public sources) before any new product surface — engineering-only, large unfed-KBQ uplift. Do the licence cost-benefit on the four Phase 2 sources with the executive sponsor — IQVIA, MMIT, AlphaSense, RedBook each turn one critically-thin KBQ comprehensive but each is six-figure annual. Write the source registry surface in parallel — every new connector needs to be visible, scoreable, and degradable in the Pulse, not buried in a JSON dump at /connectors.
