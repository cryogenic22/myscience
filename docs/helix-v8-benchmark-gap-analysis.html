The one thing that makes the benchmark work: a fully provenance-linked spine.

Every artifact traces to the evidence beneath it, and a Learn loop re-calibrates the forecasts as the world changes. Nothing is asserted without a source; nothing is static.

signal → fact → insight → scenario → decision → outcome
   ↑___________________ Learn loop: signals re-weight scenario probabilities ___________________↑

In the demo: a signal (Lilly Q1 pricing comment) feeds specific dossier facts and raises the probability of a named scenario (prior 0.50 → current 0.62), with a calibration note explaining why. That bidirectional, probabilistic linkage is the product.

The headline finding.

We are not far off — the substrate the user always insisted on is real and reusable: connectors, entity graph, knowledge layer, evidence ledger, a wired learn loop, and (as of last week) a genuinely rich dossier keystone. The benchmark exposes one structural hole that everything else hangs off: the scenario vertebra. We have no first-class, probabilistic, fact-grounded scenario object — so the spine breaks exactly where "intelligence" becomes "wargaming." Close that, wire its calibration, and the rest is richness, not architecture.
